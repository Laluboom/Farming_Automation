# Main Questions

## 1. AI Plant Intelligence System

How can we design an AI system that understands a plant from images, species data, disease signals, local environment, soil conditions, and care history, then turns that understanding into useful plant-care recommendations?

### Reference Sub-Questions

- What should the system be able to identify from an image: species, growth stage, disease, pest damage, nutrient deficiency, water stress, or general plant health?
- How can image-based diagnosis be combined with non-image data such as location, weather, soil type, watering history, and crop calendars?
- What public datasets exist for plant disease recognition, pest recognition, plant traits, soil maps, climate data, and crop water or nutrient needs?
- How reliable are current plant disease datasets when compared with real farm or garden conditions?
- Could the system explain uncertainty instead of only giving one diagnosis?
- What would a useful recommendation look like: treatment, watering adjustment, fertilizer suggestion, disease warning, or "collect more data"?
- How can the system avoid harmful advice when symptoms look similar across disease, pest, water, and nutrient problems?
- What would make this system useful for small farmers, gardeners, researchers, or automated farm equipment?

## 2. Farm Automation and Decision System

How can a farm automation system sense changing plant, soil, weather, and disease conditions, then make practical decisions about irrigation, nutrition, monitoring, and intervention with minimal human effort?

### Reference Sub-Questions

- Which farm decisions are most worth automating first: irrigation timing, fertilizer scheduling, disease alerts, pest detection, crop selection, or harvest planning?
- What sensors or inputs would be needed: camera, soil moisture, soil nutrients, pH, temperature, humidity, rainfall, sunlight, GPS, or satellite data?
- How can automation adapt to local context instead of using fixed rules for every plant or crop?
- What should the system do when data is missing, noisy, contradictory, or uncertain?
- Can the system learn from previous growing seasons and improve its decisions over time?
- How should the system balance plant health, water use, fertilizer cost, labor, yield, and environmental impact?
- What parts should be fully automated, and what parts should remain human-reviewed?
- How could this work at different scales: one plant, a home garden, a greenhouse, a small farm, or a larger field?
- What would be the simplest prototype that proves the idea without requiring expensive hardware?
