# Main Question

How can light be used as a pest-control technology to attract, repel, redirect, trap, disable, or kill insects in a targeted way without creating unnecessary harm to crops, humans, beneficial insects, or the surrounding ecosystem?

## Reference Sub-Questions

### Directing Insect Movement With Light

- Which insect species move toward light, away from light, or change direction depending on wavelength, intensity, timing, and background darkness?
- How do insects use light for navigation, orientation, mating, feeding, egg laying, escape, and habitat selection?
- Can specific wavelengths guide insects away from crops, homes, storage areas, or livestock, rather than only attracting them into traps?
- How do UV, violet, blue, green, yellow, red, infrared, and white light differ in their ability to attract or repel different insect groups?
- Can light patterns, pulsing, flickering, polarization, gradients, or moving light sources control insect movement better than constant light?
- How does the position of a light source affect insect movement: overhead, side lighting, ground-level lighting, water-surface lighting, or crop-canopy lighting?
- How do natural light conditions such as moonlight, sunrise, sunset, shade, and weather affect whether artificial light works?
- Can light be combined with smell, heat, CO2, color panels, pheromones, or airflow to make traps or barriers more effective?
- What designs would work best for different environments: fields, greenhouses, grain storage, mosquito breeding water, barns, kitchens, gardens, or warehouses?

### Lethal and Damaging Light Exposure

- What wavelengths and intensities are known to kill insects, and how do the thresholds differ between species and life stages?
- What dose matters most for insect mortality: wavelength, photon flux, exposure time, distance from source, heat, or total accumulated energy?
- In the blue-light study, why were some visible wavelengths more lethal than UV for certain insects?
- What blue-light intensities kill eggs, larvae, pupae, or adults of target pests such as mosquitoes, flies, beetles, moths, aphids, mites, or stored-grain pests?
- What exposure levels damage insect eyes, photoreceptors, nervous systems, reproductive ability, or movement before killing the insect?
- Can lower-intensity light weaken insects, reduce fertility, disrupt egg laying, or make them easier to trap without requiring full lethal exposure?
- How do lethal thresholds change when insects can hide, move away, burrow, fly, or shelter under leaves?
- How close must an LED be to the insect for the light dose to matter in real-world conditions?
- Could insects adapt or become resistant to damaging light exposure over generations?
- How can a system measure or estimate the actual light dose received by an insect instead of only measuring lamp brightness?

### Biology of Insect-Light Interaction

- What organs and cells detect light in insects: compound eyes, ocelli, larvae photoreceptors, skin-like tissues, or internal photosensitive molecules?
- Why are some insects strongly attracted to light while others avoid it?
- How do insect eyes respond differently to UV, blue, green, and longer-wavelength light?
- What biological mechanism causes blue light to harm insects: oxidative stress, reactive oxygen species, photosensitizers, tissue damage, eye damage, or nervous-system disruption?
- Why do lethal wavelengths appear to be species-specific instead of simply following the rule that shorter wavelengths are always more dangerous?
- How do habitat and life cycle affect light tolerance, for example insects that live in water, soil, stored grain, leaf undersides, or dark fruit?
- Do nocturnal and diurnal insects respond differently to artificial light pest-control systems?
- How do larvae, pupae, eggs, and adults differ in light sensitivity and movement response?
- What role does insect color, cuticle transparency, body size, or developmental stage play in absorbing harmful light?
- How does light exposure interact with insect circadian rhythm, feeding behavior, reproduction, and immune stress?

### Pest-Control System Design

- Should the goal be attraction, repulsion, redirection, sterilization, weakening, or killing?
- Which pests are the best first targets for light-based control because they are sensitive, easy to expose, or already gather near controllable locations?
- How can light-based pest control avoid harming pollinators and other beneficial insects?
- Can a smart system change wavelength, intensity, timing, and direction based on camera detection of insect species?
- What safety limits are needed for humans, animals, plants, and nearby ecosystems?
- How much energy would a useful light-based system require compared with chemical pesticides, traps, or manual control?
- What is the simplest prototype: a directional LED trap, a mosquito-water irradiation box, a greenhouse light barrier, or a grain-storage beetle control chamber?
- What measurements should be collected in experiments: attraction rate, avoidance rate, mortality, eye damage, fertility, movement speed, trap capture, crop damage, or non-target effects?
- What would prove that light is controlling insect movement rather than heat, shadow, smell, vibration, or container design?

## Starting Reference

- Hori, Shibuya, Sato, and Saito, "Lethal effects of short-wavelength visible light on insects" (`srep07383.pdf`): shows that blue-light LEDs can kill insects, but effective wavelengths and tolerance differ by species and life stage.
